PMS Dashboard

The default landing screen. Four rows, each answering a different question in a different tense: how are we doing, what must I do now, where is the trend going, what is falling through. Shown inside the shell from page 03.

Reasoning · hierarchy and trade-offs
  • Movements sit above analytics. The 30-day chart is what a manager wants; the arrivals list is what the person actually logged in at 09:00 needs. Row 2 is the widest, tallest row and carries the only one-click actions on the screen.
  • Six KPIs, one line, no icons. Occupancy first because it is the number everyone asks for. I deprioritised decoration in the cards: no icon, no card title styling, just label, figure, delta, sparkline. At 1280 they wrap to 3×2 rather than shrink the metric below 32px.
  • The room board is a board, not a table. Grouped by floor, 7-status colour, and the popover carries guest, balance and housekeeping state so the agent does not navigate away to answer a phone question.
  • What I cut: a welcome banner, a weather widget, and per-card menus. Also no auto-refresh animation — data updates in place with a quiet “updated 14:32” stamp, because a table that reflows under a cursor causes mis-clicks.

Mobile decisions

  • Property switcher moves out of the header into its own 44px strip — at 390 it cannot share a row with the hamburger and avatar without dropping below the touch minimum.
  • Movements tabs collapse from four to two segmented buttons; In-house and No-shows move into the overflow, because a manager on a phone is checking today’s traffic, not auditing.
  • Each card carries exactly one 48px primary action. No swipe-to-reveal: it hides the action and misfires while scrolling.
  • Analytics are one tap away on a separate tab rather than stacked below — eleven screens of scrolling is not a dashboard.
